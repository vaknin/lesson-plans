# Letter S (Snake): the lesson design. This is the single source of truth.

Approved by Aviv on 2026-11-17. Do not change stages, minutes, activities or words without asking him. Revised and approved on 2026-11-17: the words are taught in context through one story told live by the teacher; stage 9 is "Hiss when you hear S!".

## Facts
- Grade 3, Israeli EFL beginners, CEFR Pre-A1. They are still learning the alphabet and have already done the letter B lesson (vaknin.github.io/letter-b: book, bag, ball, bus, banana, balloon, plus the chunk "What is it? It's a ___.").
- Length: a **40-min period, about 30 net minutes** (arrival, class management, interruptions). The plan has 26 min of activities; the LP column sums to 35, with each stage given more time. Taught tomorrow, 2026-11-18.
- **Projector only.** The teacher runs the site; the kids have no laptops. **Nothing printed, nothing physical** (no cards, balls or bags).
- **No homework.**
- Teacher and site speak English first; Hebrew is support text only. No Hebrew voice.
- **Critical:** the lesson is built on **Repetition → Recognition → Reproduction**. The class teacher really cares about it. Every strand runs all three phases in that order: the spoken word first, then the written word, then the spoken sentence, then the written sentence. The phase names appear in the teacher's plan and the LP only, never on the kids' screen.
- **Do not practise writing the letter** (no air-writing, no tracing, no stroke animation).
- **Differentiation inside each task:** the easier version is the word only ("Snake?"); the harder version is the full chunk ("Is it a snake?" / "Yes, it is!" / "No, it isn't!"). No levels, no placement quiz, no race.

## Target language
Key word: **Snake** (S says "sss", like a snake).

| star | כוכב | hands open and close (twinkle) | Sam cuts a star. |

### The story: "Sam's Day at School" (approved)
All 6 words are taught inside one short story, never as a random list. **The teacher tells it live**; the screen shows one picture panel per line plus the line's text, with the S word highlighted. There is no voice clip for story lines.
1. This is Sam. Sam goes to school. (school)
2. Sam has scissors. Snip, snip! (scissors)
3. Sam cuts a star. (star)
4. Look! A snake! Sssss! (snake)
5. "Stop, snake! Stop!" (stop)
6. The snake is sad. It has no friends. (sad)
7. "Here, snake. A star for you!" The snake is happy!

Keep instructions minimal: these are 3rd graders who are not native speakers. The teacher demonstrates each activity instead of explaining it.

Sentence chunk (B3 and the communicative task): "Is it a snake?" / "Is it scissors?" / "Is it a star?" / "Is it sad?" / "Is it a school?" / "Is it stop?" → "Yes, it is!" / "No, it isn't!"
Prior chunk: "What is it? It's a ___." (from the letter B lesson).

Totals: net 26 min, LP 35 min. Repetition/Recognition/Reproduction stages 3–10 = 17 of 26 net min. The LP's Production rows: B1 says "no separate step" (pupils choose and say the words freely in the pair game; this departs from F3 s.9, which lists production as its own stage, to fit the net minutes); B2 names the 2 silent items of Which word?.

**Pictures:** each of the six word pictures shows one thing. "Sad" is a sad face, not the sad snake.

**Transitions and praise (every stage):** one fixed signal for every stage change: "Stop!" with the palm (the lesson's own word and gesture), then "Look!". One specific praise line per stage (written in the teacher's plan), e.g. "Row 2, great gestures!".

**Teacher keys (the site):** Space = the next step on this screen (the tiny legend, bottom-left, names it; Space never changes the item or the stage). → / ← = next / previous item inside the stage, never the stage (at the last item the legend flashes "PgDn next part"). PgDn / PgUp, the top-bar < >, the agenda icons, 1–9 / 10–12 and the menu change the stage. R hear the last line again · F full screen · M menu. Stage letters: S STOP! (Stop me when) · X swap (Is it a…?, pair game) · Y show it all (Peek!) · C new row (Snake chain). A held key does not repeat; a key that can't act now is greyed in the legend, and pressing it flashes the legend.

**If running late**, in this order: (1) Which word? → 4 items, keeping the 2 silent ones; (2) Stop me when… → 3 targets; (3) Snake chain → one chain by the whole class, one error fixed. Never trim What's missing?, Peek! or the pair game. Clock rule: the pair game starts at least 8 minutes before the bell.

**Story claim (exam):** justify the story by F3 s.9, F2 s.6, L-F p. 131 and the evaluation form. Do not claim research shows words are learned more easily in a story: Goossens et al. (2014) found the opposite for one such procedure, which is why each word is still taught with its own picture, gesture and repetition.

## Research basis. Cite the slide or page, not summaries.md
Checked against the primary files by the research review (notes/fable/2026-11-17-letter-s-research-check.md).
- **F3 s.9** (What is Practice): "The practice stage should include: repetition, recognition, reproduction and production stages … cover all 4 modes of communication … be in CONTEXT … proceed from easy to difficult, from mechanical to meaningful, from non-communicative to communicative, from accuracy to fluency, controlled to free." This is the backbone of the lesson and the source of the story context. F3 s.4: verbalization = the teacher describes and demonstrates. F3 s.5: automatization. F3 s.6: autonomy.
- **F2** (Practice+Language+and+CLT.pptx): s.6 contextualization is a basic principle; s.10–11 controlled practice (accuracy, teacher control); s.21 matching; s.14–15 sentence completion; s.23–24 story chains; s.28–29 games (charades, 20 questions); s.30 "don't show each other their information. They have to talk"; s.31 closed vs open gap; s.34 the teacher decides between a specific structure and free language; s.42 communicative work comes after the language is taught and after oral→written sentence level; s.46 the 7 criteria.
- **L-F** (Larsen-Freeman 2000): p. 129 Morrow's information gap, choice and feedback; p. 129 "tightly controlled… one way" is not communicative; p. 127 and p. 132 errors noted and returned to later; p. 132 judicious L1.
- Guess my mime is a **closed information-gap game** (F2 s.31), the least controlled practice in the lesson, not free production. The sources are silent on minute splits.
- No peer-reviewed study tests the named R→R→R sequence; its parts have support (recognition is easier than recall, retrieval practice, gestures plus pictures with 8-year-olds). See the review, section E.

## Lesson objective (one sentence)
By the end of the lesson, pupils can recognise and say 6 words that begin with S (snake, scissors, star, sad, school, stop) in the context of a short story ("Sam's Day at School"), recognise and read them in writing, and ask and answer "Is it a…? Yes, it is / No, it isn't" in a guessing game with a partner.
